Cardiovascular-kidney-metabolic (CKM) syndrome is increasingly framed as a **multisystem pathobiologic network** rather than a simple clustering of obesity, diabetes, chronic kidney disease, and cardiovascular disease. The 2023 American Heart Association Presidential Advisory formalized CKM as a clinical construct, but the mechanistic literature makes clear that CKM is fundamentally a disorder of **inter-organ amplification**, in which dysfunctional adiposity, insulin resistance, neurohormonal activation, endothelial injury, inflammation, and renal dysfunction reinforce one another over time.<sup>[1]</sup>

## 1. Dysfunctional Adiposity as an Upstream Driver

A central initiating feature of CKM is **dysfunctional adiposity**. Visceral and ectopic fat depots act as active endocrine and inflammatory tissues, promoting insulin resistance, altered adipokine signaling, oxidative stress, and chronic low-grade inflammation. These metabolic abnormalities also intersect with sympathetic and RAAS activation, thereby linking obesity to sodium retention, vascular dysfunction, and increasing hemodynamic stress.<sup>[1,2]</sup>

## 2. Hemodynamics as a Biologic Amplifier

Within CKM, hemodynamic stress is not merely a parallel risk marker. It acts as a **biologic amplifier** that converts upstream metabolic dysfunction into structural cardiovascular and renal injury. Progressive blood pressure elevation contributes to arterial remodeling, myocardial load, and kidney damage, while obesity- and diabetes-associated renal stress accelerates albuminuria and decline in glomerular filtration. This helps explain why CKM progression is often characterized by tightly coupled cardiovascular and renal deterioration.<sup>[1,2,5]</sup>

## 3. Inflammation as an Inter-Organ Signaling Hub

Recent work increasingly identifies inflammation as a **molecular hub** of CKM progression. NF-κB, JAK-STAT, PI3K-AKT, Wnt, and PPAR-related pathways have all been implicated in the inflammatory crosstalk that links adipose dysfunction, endothelial injury, myocardial remodeling, and renal fibrosis. These signaling networks support the concept that CKM is sustained by self-reinforcing feedback loops rather than isolated organ-specific pathology.<sup>[2,3]</sup>

## 4. Lipid Markers Require Metabolic Context

CKM also highlights an important limitation of conventional biomarker interpretation: **context matters**. In diabetes, HDL can become glycated, oxidized, and functionally impaired, with reduced cholesterol efflux and anti-inflammatory capacity. Accordingly, the biologic meaning of HDL-related markers may differ across glycemic states, and simple concentration-based interpretation may miss important aspects of lipoprotein dysfunction.<sup>[4]</sup>

## 5. Mechanism-Based Therapy in CKM

The modern CKM framework is clinically useful because it aligns with **pathway-directed therapy**. SGLT2 inhibitors, now foundational in contemporary CKD care, appear to provide benefit beyond glucose lowering through restoration of tubuloglomerular feedback, reduction of intraglomerular pressure, natriuresis, and broader cardio-renal unloading. This mechanistic breadth is precisely why they occupy an increasingly central position in high-risk CKM management.<sup>[1,5]</sup>

## 6. Our Cohort Adds a Temporal Layer

Against this broader mechanistic backdrop, our longitudinal cohort study suggests that CKM progression may often follow a dominant **metabolic → hemodynamic → renal** trajectory, with stronger forward than reverse pathways over time. We also observed that tighter coupling between blood pressure worsening and renal decline marked more accelerated phenotypes, supporting the view that CKM is not only a syndrome of coexisting disease, but also a syndrome of **ordered temporal progression**.

## Conclusion

Taken together, the literature supports a model in which CKM is driven by **networked progression**: dysfunctional adiposity initiates metabolic injury, hemodynamic and neurohormonal stress amplify damage, inflammation stabilizes the pathologic circuit, and renal dysfunction feeds back into cardiovascular burden. A mechanism-based interpretation of CKM may therefore offer greater clinical insight than static organ-based classification alone.
